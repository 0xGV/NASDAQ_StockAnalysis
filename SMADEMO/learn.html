Technical Indicator: Some signal based on patterns based on price, volume, and/or interest

The following is a list of the technical indicators available on this website.

  • Moving Average (MA): Calculation that smooths out price action data. This creates an average price line that can be used to indicate trends.
    • Simple Moving Average (SMA): Uses a basic average with no weights. If n is the number of periods, A is the average in a single period i, then
      • SMA = A0 + A1 + A2 ... An n
    • Exponential Moving Average (EMA): Moving average that uses weights as well as a smoothing factor to give higher weight to more recent prices. This indicator can be more accurate when there is recent volatility.
      • EMA=( 2 n+1 ) * (Close-Previous EMA) + Previous EMA

  • Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): Technical tool used to discover bullish and bearish signals in the market. Often, the MACD is displayed with a histogram that graphs the difference between the MACD and its signal line. This histogram can be used to identify when bullish or bearish momentum is high.
    • MACD=12-Period EMA-26-Period EMA

  • Bollinger Bands®: Tool that plots a positive and negative multiple of the standard deviation away from a moving average of a security's price. This moving average could be a simple or exponential moving average. This can be used to help discover when an asset is oversold or overbought.
    • If the price is in the upper end of the bands, it is likely to fall back toward the MA.
    • If the price is in the lower end of the bands, it is likely to move up toward the MA.

  • Relative Strength Index (RSI): Oscillator used to analyze an asset to help determine if the stock is overbought or oversold. The graph ranges from 0 to 100.
    • Over 70 or 80 is considered overbought
    • Below 20 or 30 is considered oversold

Fundamental data

Fundamental data includes all data that is involved with the value of a stock. This could include things like cash flow, revenue, profit retention, etc.
Fundamental analysis involves using this data to make decisions on this data.

Quote Data

  • Open: The price of a single share of a stock at the beginning of some period.
  • Close: The price of a single share of a stock at the end of some period.
  • High: The greatest price of a single share of a stock in some period.
  • Low: The smallest price of a single share of a stock in some period.

Valuation Metrics

  • Book Value: Cost of carraying an asset on the company's balance sheet.
  • Trailing P/E: Relative valuation ratio based on the last 12 months of earnings.
  • Forward P/E: Relative valuation ratio estimating the next 12 months of earnings.
  • Price-to-Sales (P/S) Ratio: Compares a company's stock price to its revenue.
  • Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio: Compares a company's market cap. to its book value.
  • EV to Revenue (EV/R): Enterprise value-to-revenue multiple. Compares the company's enterprise value to its revenue.
  • EV to EBITDA (EV/R): Measure of a company's return on investment.

Income Data

  • Revenue: The amount of money a company earns in a specific period. This is before taxes and expenses are considered.
  • Revenue per share: Revenue divided by the number of outstanding shares.
  • Quarterly Revenue Growth: How much a company's sales increased in one quarter comapred to the previous.
  • Gross Profit: The amount of money made by a company after deducting liabilities.
  • EBITDA: Acronym for: Earnings before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization Used to measure the company's financial performance.
  • Profit Margin: Ratio to measure a company's ability to make money. For example, if a business lists a 15% profit margin in the last quarter, its net income is $0.15 per dollar of revenue.
  • Operating Margin: Similar to a profit margin, except it accounts for variable costs like wages or material costs. Higher ratios are better (because it shows the company can turn sales into profit)
  • Return on Assets (ROA): Indicator for the profitability of a company relative to its total assets.
    ROA= Net Income Total Assets
    The higher the value, the better.
  • Return on Equity (ROE): Typically used with the ROA, it is the net income divided by the shareholder equity:
    ROE= Net Income Shareholder Equity
    It is expressed as a percentage, and should be calculated if net income & equity are both positive. This value should be compared to similar companies to judge its performance in the sector.
  • Earnings-per-share (EPS): A value that is the company's profit divided by the number of outstanding shares of its common stock. This can be used to measure the company's profitability. A strong earnings can mean the stock will grow, while a weak earnings or even a loss can indicate the stock price may fall.
  • Diluted EPS: Calculation used to assess a company's EPS assuming all convertible securities were exercised. Typically lower than the standard EPS.
  • Quarterly Earnings Growths: How much a company's earnings increased in one quarter comapred to the previous.

Trading Information

  • Market Cap (sometimes, market value): The open market valuation of the company. It is the value of the asset with regards to the marketplace.
  • P/E ratio: Price-to-earnings ratio. This is used to value a company by measuring its current share price relative to its per-share earnings (EPS)
  • PEG ratio: Price/earnings to growth ratio. Used to assess a company's value and factor in its expected earning's growth.
  • Beta: Measures the volatility of a security compared to the market. Can be used to show if a stock moves in the same direction as the market or not.
    A value...
    • Equal to 1.0 implies the stock is strongly correlated with the market.
    • Less than 1.0 implies the stock is (supposedly) less volatile compared to the market. This implies lowered systematic risk.
    • Greater than 1.0 implies the stock is (supposedly) more volatile compared to the market. This implies higher systematic risk.
  • 52-week high: The highest recorded price in a 52 week period.
  • 52-week low: The lowest recorded price in a 52 week period.

Dividend & Split information

  • Dividend per share: Sum of declared dividends for every outstanding share.
  • Dividend yield: Ratio of dividend to price, showing how much a company pays out in dividends relative to its stock price (annually).
  • Forward Annual Dividend Rate: Estimation of the dividend rate.
  • Forward Annual Dividend Yield: Estimation of the annual dividend expressed as a percentage of the current stock price.
  • Payout ratio: Ratio of the total amount of dividends paid relative to the company's net income.
  • Dividend date: When the dividend is due to be paid.
  • Ex-Dividend date: Date on or after which a security is traded without a previously declared divided/distribution.
  • Last split factor: The ratio of the most recent stock split. For instance, a factor of 5:1 means that the number of shares increased by a factor of 5. Consequently, the stock price decreased by a factor of 5.
  • Last split date: When the most recent stock split occurred.

Share Statistics

  • Shares Outstanding: Total number of shares issued and actively held by stockholders. This includes insiders and outsiders.
  • Shares Float: Similar to the shares outstanding, except it excludes shares held by insiders or controlling investors.
  • Shares Short: Approximate number of shares in short positions. Short positions occur when a trader sells a security with the intention of rebuying it, or covering it later at a lower price.
  • Shares Short prior month: The amount of shares short from the previous month.
  • Short ratio: Ratio used to determine if a stock is heavily shorted or not. Calculated as the ratio between the shares short and the stock's average daily volume.
  • % Insiders: Percent of shares owned by insiders at a company. Insiders are people (typically managers, owners, founders, etc.) that own stock in the company. Used as how management views the company. A higher percentage is good, as it implies management believes the future outlook is good.
    Calculated as the ratio of the shares owned by insiders to the shares outstanding.
  • % Institutions: Percent of shares owned by large institutions (banks). Similar to % insiders, a higher percentage is good, implying large institutions believe the future outlook is good.

This site's StockPicker is a tool of our own design to decide if a stock is a strong buy, weak buy, weak sell, strong sell, or neutral if a conclusion cannot be made.

The StockPicker takes into account several different properties of a single stock, focusing primarily on its technical data.

Technical data considered:

  • RSI overbought/oversold signals -- The system uses 70/30 for indicators. The more time the stock resides in a zone, the more impact it has on the StockPicker's index
  • MACD signals -- The system focuses on crossover events
  • Trends of a stock (over the lifetime of a stock) -- The more a stock spends on an uptrend, the higher the score. Of course, the more a stock spends on a downtrend, the lower the score.
